ABOUT

Hope is a multidisciplinary artist whose creative practice thrives at the intersection of visual expression, physical discipline, and grounded connection to the earth. Rather than confining themself to a single medium, Hope describes their life as an “organized chaos,” a philosophy that permeates their diverse portfolio of work in painting, photography, and performance.

As a painter, Hope utilizes colour as a visceral language, seeking to convey emotions that words often fail to capture. their work is deeply rooted in a feminist perspective, serving as a lens through which they challenge patriarchal narratives, deconstructs stereotypes, and reclaims space for underrepresented voices. For Hope, art is not merely aesthetic; it is an act of revelation that asks who is being represented and who benefits from specific narratives. Their goal is simple yet profound: to make viewers feel seen, validating their stories and perspectives through a sense of shared human connection.

Photography complements their painting by documenting reality while revealing layers beneath the surface, capturing moments that speak louder than visual appearance alone. Beyond the studio, Hope’s creative energy extends into their hobbies, which they view as essential components of their artistic identity. Gardening serves as their grounding practice, teaching their patience and the value of slow, careful growth, acting as lessons that inform their intense creative periods. Similarly, their dedication to yoga and aerial arts provides the physical strength and mental clarity necessary to sustain their artistic output.

Hope finds inspiration in the details of everyday life, whether through reading widely, walking in nature, or engaging with their community. Currently immersed in the works of Anne Rice and Pema Chödrön, they appreciate the interplay between source material and adaptation, a nuance that reflects their broader interest in narrative and interpretation. Whether creating a painting, tending to their garden, or exploring the skies on aerial silks, Hope remains committed to fostering community and celebrating diverse perspectives. Their life’s work is a testament to the power of creation to comfort, challenge, and reveal the intention behind our existence.

Surviving and living with sexual assault, Hope wields colour as a visceral language to reclaim space stolen by patriarchal violence and systemic indifference, turning canvas into an act of revelation about who benefits from silence. Each brushstroke is a refusal to be unseen and a way of saying "I survived, and my story belongs here".
